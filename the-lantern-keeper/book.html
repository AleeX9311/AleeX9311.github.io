About the book

A light with a job

The Lantern Keeper is a novel about Edith Voss, who inherits a harbor light that was never meant to call ships home. Grayharbor is small enough that everyone knows the tide table and large enough to misplace a promise.

The book moves through one autumn. There is a net-mender who will not sell her work, a post office that closes for lunch, and a ledger of nights when the lamp stayed dark on purpose. It is not a ghost story, though the town would prefer that explanation.

Sample chapter

Edith checked the wick the way other people check a lock: once, then again, because the first look is only a wish. The glass was clean. The oil was honest. Out on the water there was nothing to warn and nothing to welcome, which was the condition her grandmother had called a fair night.

“Leave it,” her brother said from the stairs. He had come up without his coat, which meant he planned to be brief. “They can see the town from the road. They don’t need us announcing it.”

Edith struck the match anyway. A town that only lit itself in a storm started to believe it was braver than it was. The flame took, small and ordinary, and the beam laid a pale road on the black water. Somewhere below, a door shut. Someone had decided the conversation was over. The light did not agree.

By morning the ledger had one new line. Clear night. Lamp lit. No ship. Edith sanded the ink and did not add the argument. The book was for the weather, not for the family.
