Features

Only the work that gets sent

Six jobs. No charts for their own sake.

  • 01

    Proposals

    A letter the client can answer, with the price in the same place as the work.

  • 02

    Invoices

    A due date, a total, and a status that is either open or paid.

  • 03

    Client notes

    The last conversation stays with the client, not in a chat you have to search.

  • 04

    A plain timeline

    What you sent, and when, without a dashboard of graphs.

  • 05

    Quiet reminders

    A nudge before a due date. Off, if you would rather remember yourself.

  • 06

    A file you can export

    A copy of the work that does not depend on the product staying online.

Questions

Before you take it seriously

Is Orchard Desk a real product?

No. It is a sample landing page in a web developer’s portfolio. The product, the prices, and the testimonials are fictional.

Will a button charge me or create an account?

No. A button only changes a note in this browser. Nothing is stored and nothing is billed.

Can a real product use this structure?

Yes. The page is a pattern: a promise, features, prices, proof, questions, and a next step.

Where should a real call to action go?

To a short form, a booking link, or an email. On a client site, that link would be a real one.

Who made this page?

Aleks Kmetec. The credit is in the footer, and it links back to the portfolio.
